The grandmother gets everyone in her family killed — and Flannery O'Connor's point is that she's you. A full analysis of A Good Man Is Hard to Find.

Every story in this collection is a machine built to catch the reader. O'Connor lures you into judging a shallow character by the exact cheap yardstick that character uses — good manners, good breeding, being "a good man" — lets you feel superior for pages, then springs a violence that reveals real grace has nothing to do with any of it. The title isn't a lament. It's the con.

We work through the title story beat by beat: Red Sammy's barbecue joint, where "good" only ever means "on my side"; the navy hat with white violets the grandmother wears so a corpse would still read as a lady; the secret panel she invents, which turns the car down the fatal dirt road; and the Misfit — a murderer in silver-rimmed spectacles who has thought harder about the Resurrection than anyone in church. Then the moment of grace: "You're one of my own children," answered with three shots, and the line that holds O'Connor's whole ethic — "She would have been a good woman if it had been somebody there to shoot her every minute of her life."

The same trap runs through the rest of the collection. Hulga, the philosophy PhD who believes in nothing, loses her wooden leg to a Bible salesman who out-nihilists her. Mr. Shiftlet preaches about the mystery of the human spirit while abandoning a disabled girl at a diner. The grotesque bodies aren't shock value — they're O'Connor making an invisible spiritual condition visible: "to the hard of hearing you shout, and for the almost-blind you draw large, startling figures."

We also take the hardest objections seriously. Is violence-as-grace really grace, or an author playing a sadistic God with a stacked deck? And the casual racism of the grandmother and her era sits in the text without comment — is O'Connor exposing it or indulging it?

Spoilers throughout. Best for readers studying the collection for class or a book club — and for anyone who suspects they're too smart to fall for it.
