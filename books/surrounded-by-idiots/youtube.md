What if the reason you feel surrounded by idiots is that you're broadcasting on the wrong frequency — and everyone else thinks YOU'RE the idiot?

This is a deep dive into "Surrounded by Idiots" by Thomas Erikson and the four-color system (Red, Yellow, Green, Blue) built on the DISC assessment. The argument is simple and uncomfortable: almost every conflict at work and home is not stupidity — it's a color collision. You speak your native behavioral language and judge everyone else for failing to understand it. Learn to read the color, translate your message, and the "idiots" disappear.

We walk through all four types with the book's own cases — Stuart, the feared Red boss whose company checks the lamp above his door; Marita, pure Yellow, telling stories on the sofa; the Green colleague who says yes and then silently refuses to row; the Blue analyst who needs fifty pages of appendix before a meeting. Then the stress masks: Red explodes, Yellow deflects, Green freezes, Blue goes cold. We also take on the book's biggest criticism head-on — four colors is blunt DISC repackaged with thin science — and ask whether it still works as a tool.
