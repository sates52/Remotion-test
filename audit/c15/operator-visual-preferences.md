## 2. 8Y (C12-B) — detailed directing analysis

- **Main message:** "the winner gets the reward" — a transfer. It shows the first half of the loop (reward goes to winner) literally; the "runaway / loops back" half is NOT drawn (no return arrow, no second cycle). It is still the strongest-liked clip.
- **Composition:** one locked wide frame; inset rounded white card over a softened sunburst/office set. Three entities on one horizontal line, evenly spaced, left->right = cause->medium->recipient, i.e. it reads like a sentence (COMPETITION -> REWARD -> WINNERS). Upper half of the card is empty: room for the arrow arc.
- **Object / character choice:** two universally legible OBJECT icons (trophy, $ coin) + one person. The person is the only human; he is the recipient, so the eye ends on a face. Contrast: C12-A uses only the man.
- **Visual metaphor:** trophy = competition, coin = reward — dictionary-level, no decoding needed. The metaphor IS the mechanism: the reward is a physical thing that can change owners.
- **How the relation is told:** (1) cause ignites (grey->gold, ring), (2) arrow + travelling dot announce direction, (3) the coin itself moves along the line to the winner. The token that travels is the content, not an abstract dot — the only liked clip where the effect is a TRANSFER between two entities rather than a state change of one.
- **Size & placement:** icons ~1/4 of card height, equal size; coin finishes overlapping the winner's right side, slightly lower, reading as "in his hand / next to him". Labels under each icon, same baseline.
- **Colour & contrast:** everything starts desaturated grey/navy; exactly ONE warm accent appears (gold trophy + gold ring) on a cream card. Gold is semantically right for "win". Coin stays grey (accent is not over-spent).
- **Camera & motion:** none on the camera. Motion is restrained to three discrete events, each with a hold before/after. Nothing idles.
- **The visible change:** two before/after marks at once — the dashed empty REWARD box (where it was) and the coin at the winner (where it is). Same grammar as the dashed old-level line in tanks: the past state stays visible.
- **Text:** three single-word uppercase labels, small, sans; no headline, no sentence. Contrast C12-A: a 6-word headline THE MEANS TO COMPETE EVEN MORE.
- **Sound-off legibility:** high: "competition -> winners get the reward" is readable from the last frame alone. ("Even more / loop" is not.)
- **Frame-by-frame vs C12-A:** A = one man, arms raised in triumph, mouth moving, against full sunburst set; text types in at ~1 s and then nothing changes for ~9 s. Every A frame is the same picture; B has a beginning (all grey), a middle (gold + arrow) and an end (coin moved, slot empty).
- **The distinctive element:** the empty dashed slot. The scene shows something LEAVING one place and ARRIVING at another, so the change is undeniable and has a story shape, using objects anyone recognises.

## 3. Why each was preferred (hypotheses) and the concrete difference from the alternative

- **C12-B over A:** A is a static celebrating character + text (a "talking head with caption"); B has a 3-step event and a transfer. Hypothesis: the operator rewards *something happens on screen that matches the sentence*.
- **C06-B over A:** A = two characters, Darrow pointing, headline DARROW DESPISES THEM, little change over 12 s. B = flame ignites grey->orange, face turns angry. Hypothesis: clean single cause object + visible emotional payoff beats a two-person stand-off with a label doing the work.
- **C09-B over A:** A = tiny figure (~1/6 frame height) in a big empty classroom, headline THE WORLD IS WAITING then struck through in red. B = cap turns red, student smiles (the "delusion" smile). Hypothesis: A's figure is too small and the payoff is a text effect; B's payoff is a face.
- **C05-B over A:** A = two characters standing (one holding a small book), headline SIMULATING ANOTHER CONSCIOUSNESS; nothing visibly changes. B = red book, empathy tank fills with a dashed "before" line. Hypothesis: an abstract outcome (empathy) became a measurable level.
- **C15-A over B:** B = a plain quote card ("Satellite imagery shows ... compound."). A = a huge black dog. Hypothesis: the noun of the sentence drawn BIG beats any text; quote cards are disliked (see §4).
- **C10 both:** A = bold graphic (big black pictograms, one orange individual, slow push-in, headline). B = the same orange-individual-in-tribe motif + a draining tank. Hypothesis: both share the same strong iconography (black/orange, high-contrast, no faces), so both look "designed"; A for its graphic boldness and slow scale growth, B for its event.

## 4. Common strengths, and where the seven diverge

Common to all seven:
- **A single dominant concrete subject per entity** (trophy, coin, flame, cap, book, tank, dog, pictogram tribe). No liked clip relies on a bare set or a two-person dialogue shot.
- **One saturated accent on a neutral field** (gold / orange / red on cream, white or grey). Background stays quiet.
- **Little or no running text**: labels of 1-2 words, or one short headline (C10-A, C15-A). Never a sentence on screen.
- **Large, high-contrast shapes** (black silhouettes, dark tank fill, black dog).

Divergence:
- **Event vs. static:** five liked clips are B events; C10-A and C15-A have no cause->effect event at all. What they share with the Bs is not the relation grammar but iconic big subject + accent + calm frame. C15-A's "event" is an entrance; C10-A's is only a push-in.
- **Faces:** C06-B, C09-B, C12-B, C15-A have a person; C05-B, C10-A/B have none. Faces are not required.
- **Text headline:** present in both liked As, absent in all liked Bs. So a headline is not disliked per se — when the picture alone is strong.

Contrast — the four unpicked pairs:
- **Quote cards were never chosen (0/4: C01-B, C07-B, C08-B, C15-B).** Every quote-card version lost or tied in "neither".
- Unpicked production As are people-only or empty: C01-A extreme close-up of two cropped faces in a library; C07-A an EMPTY library set + headline A PROFOUNDLY PRIVATE (no subject at all); C08-A two similar men, one pointing, + INVISIBLE BORDERS; C04-A one small figure on a yellow field + KNOCKED OFF BY FAILURE.
- **C04-B is the instructive miss:** same grammar as C06-B (cause icon + ring + arrow + face neutral->sad) but not chosen. Visible differences: the cloud is dark from the start (no grey->colour ignition, only the rain turns yellow); the ring is yellow on a yellow card over a yellow set (accent = background hue, near-zero pop); the face change is small. Hypothesis: the single-relation grammar only "lands" when the accent contrasts with the field and the cause visibly ignites.

## 5. Generalisable principles

1. **Draw the sentence's nouns as recognisable objects, not people talking.**
   Evidence: trophy/coin, flame, cap, book, dog all liked; C01-A/C08-A (talking faces) and C07-A (empty set) not.
   Apply: author `object`/icon for the cause and for the thing affected; keep the person as recipient only.
   Risk: abstract nouns with no honest icon (C07 "persona", C01 "spiritual deformity") — a forced icon misleads; those failed in both forms.
2. **One accent colour, earned by the cause, on a neutral field.**
   Evidence: grey->gold/orange/red in every liked B; C04-B yellow-on-yellow lost.
   Apply: renderer: start cause icon desaturated, ignite to the book palette accent; refuse/flag an accent within ~30° hue of the set background.
   Risk: palettes that are themselves warm/saturated (C04 yellow) need the card or set desaturated first.
3. **Show before AND after in the same frame.**
   Evidence: dashed old level (C05-B, C10-B), dashed empty slot (C12-B).
   Apply: keep the ghost marker for any level/position change; prefer tank/level or move over face-only effects when the effect is a quantity.
   Risk: a ghost line on a qualitative effect (an attitude) is fake precision.
4. **Motion restraint with a story shape (still -> ignite -> travel -> changed -> hold).**
   Evidence: liked Bs all have 3 discrete events and a locked camera; C10-A's single slow push-in also liked. Static-for-10-s As (C12-A, C05-A, C08-A) lost.
   Apply: one camera move max; events on narration cues; no idle loops.
   Risk: a 4-s grey opening may read as dead air in a fast passage (C12-B/C06-B both open ~4 s still).
5. **Text: labels ≤ 2 words; a headline only when the picture is already strong; never a sentence card.**
   Evidence: quote cards 0/4; liked As have a picture carrying the meaning plus short headline.
   Risk: names/quotes that ARE the content (a direct speech line) may still need text.
6. **Scale: the subject must be big.** C09-A tiny figure and C04-A small figure lost; C15-A dog and C10-A pictograms (growing) liked.
   Risk: scaling up a weak subject (empty set) does not help.

## 6. Do NOT generalise

- The black dog's size and orange collar (C15-A) — specific to a literal noun in the narration.
- The orange-centre-figure-in-black-tribe pictogram (C10) — farsighted's own motif; good because it recurs in that book, not as a universal "individual" sign.
- Gold = winning (C12) and red cap = school (C09) — colours chosen for meaning; do not hard-code gold/red.
- Smile = delusion (C09-B) only works because the narration says delusion; a smile is not a generic "effect".
- Coin-transfer (C12-B): good only where the narration literally gives something to someone.
- The inset white card itself — present in liked Bs and in disliked quote cards; it is not the cause of liking.

## 7. Directing decisions to try on NEW books (existing Antidote features)

1. Causal beat with a quantity effect -> single-relation, cause **object icon** left, **tank/level** right, dashed old level; labels 1 word each.
2. Causal beat where something is given/taken -> three entities in a row (giver-object, the thing, receiver person); the token that travels is the thing itself; leave the dashed empty slot.
3. Attitude/emotion effect -> cause icon + person, face change must be a strong pair (neutral->angry/smile), and the accent must contrast with the set (check palette; desaturate warm sets inside the card).
4. Fact beat naming a concrete noun (dog, letter, gun) -> draw that noun LARGE with a pointing/looking person; short headline allowed. Do not use a quote card.
5. Concept beat with a recurring book motif -> bold pictogram + one accent + one slow push-in (C10-A pattern), headline ≤ 4 words.
6. Avoid: empty-set + headline (C07-A), cropped dialogue close-ups (C01-A), two similar people pointing (C08-A), quote cards for narration that can be drawn.
7. Use 8Y as a reference for clarity of stages, not as a template: vary entity count (2 or 3), effect type (level, move, face, appearance) per beat to avoid a repetitive look.
