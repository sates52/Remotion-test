The kindest thing anyone ever told you about your writing is that nobody wants to read it.

In Nobody Wants to Read Your Sh*t, Steven Pressfield draws on his years as a 1960s Madison Avenue copywriter, a starving Hollywood screenwriter and the author of The War of Art to make one blunt argument: the reader is not your audience, the reader is your adversary — and self-expression is the problem. The writer's real job is empathy turned into structure.

In this deep dive we unpack concept (De Beers' "A diamond is forever", Die Hard), theme (the Iliad, The Sopranos, Seinfeld), Hollywood's three acts, why the villain makes the hero (Wall Street's Gordon Gekko, Chinatown), the inciting incident, why non-fiction is a story too, and the professional mindset — then we take on the book's hardest criticism: can structure become a straitjacket?

For writers, screenwriters, marketers, students and anyone sitting on a white whale of a project.
